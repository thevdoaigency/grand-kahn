School buses don't get extensions.

Most deadlines in this business are contract dates. This one was the first day of school.

Our crew put in EV charging and a solar carport for a local school district this summer. The date wasn't negotiable — kids were getting picked up in August whether we were done or not.

That's really all "critical load" means. Not a buzzword. Just power that has to work on the day it matters — for a hospital, a manufacturing line, or a bus yard full of kids.

We were done early.

Source: Sam, INTV 01:26 — "They got school starting in the beginning of August and we need to be done by that."

~95 words

We don't turn anything on until QC says so.

There's a moment at the end of a solar job where everything's mounted, the wire's pulled, and it would be very easy to just... flip it on.

We don't.

Wire management. Labeling. Torque checks. Quality control, front to back — before a single string gets energized.

Anyone can turn a system on. The job is knowing it'll stay on. That part doesn't show up in the drone shot, which is exactly why I'm telling you about it.

Source: Jeff, INTV 01:41 — "…we'll be starting our quality control here pretty soon before we start plugging anything in or turning anything on."

The carport pays the district back.

The solar carport we built this summer doesn't just shade the buses it charges.

It back-feeds the district's electrical service — generating power that offsets what the fleet draws. Public money, working in both directions.

Our general foreman put it plainly on camera: it "helps take some of the stress off of their finances."

EV charging that funds itself a little more every sunny day. That's the whole idea.
