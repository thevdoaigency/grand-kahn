GRAND KAHN ELECTRIC

Mike Casto — LinkedIn Launch Posts

Four posts to open Mike's page and stake a claim to a category — critical power: the loads that can't fail, not "electrical contractor." Each one moves him toward being the person you call when the power isn't allowed to go down.

The voice
He sells by refusing to oversell. Modest openers, named clients and years doing the work, first person, no hashtags, no stacked adjectives. His one move per post is the quiet pivot — the humble line that lands as the flex, never a hard close. Where Rob ends on a flash of heat, Mike ends on plain resolve.
The hard rule
Mike doesn't publish the longevity claim loose. "119 years · since 1907 · Chicago's first registered electrical contractor" is the load-bearing credential — and the live site currently says 1907, 1928, and "117 years" in three different places.
Publish gate. Lock the founding year to one verified source and confirm "first registered" before any post using it goes live. Every draft below leans on it. Breaking this once undoes the credibility the whole page runs on.
Second gate
The named-client posts (Rush, City Hope, Tesla) need each client's OK to name publicly, and the Aurora school district stays unnamed — the crew deliberately kept it off camera. Confirm clearances before Post 2 and Post 4 publish.

The Category

Flag in the ground — who Mike is

We're not the biggest electrical contractor in Chicago. We've just been here the longest. 119 years. Since 1907. Long enough to learn that the job isn't square footage — it's the load that isn't allowed to fail. An office can lose power for an afternoon. A cancer center in the middle of a scan cannot. A hospital cannot. The buildings we care most about are the ones where the power staying on isn't a convenience — it's the whole point. That's the work we've quietly built our name on. Not the biggest jobs in the city. The ones that can't afford to go dark.
NoteThe anchor line is his own, verbatim from the June 30 shoot — a modest opener that's secretly the flex. The specifics (119 years, a scan) carry it; no adjectives needed. Uses the longevity claim — gated on founding-year confirmation.

The Stakes

Who's inside the building
111 words · copy
MC
Mike Casto
President · Grand Kahn Electric
Think about what's happening inside a cancer center at any given hour. Someone is getting a scan that will tell them what the next year of their life looks like. Someone is in treatment that can't be paused. The machines doing that work don't run on good intentions — they run on power that has to be there, every second, without a flicker. That's the part of this job that doesn't show up in a photo. A finished electrical room looks the same whether it was done right or barely. The difference only shows up on the worst day — when everything else fails and the power doesn't. We build for that day.
NoteRestraint is what makes it land — no sentimentality, the facts do the emotional work, then Mike steps back to plain resolve. Parallel to Rob's stakes post. No client specifics, so this one is clear to publish on its own.

The Forward Flag

Where the work is going
117 words · copy
MC
Mike Casto
President · Grand Kahn Electric
The loads that can't fail are multiplying. A school district we just finished a project for has a fleet of electric buses that has to roll on the first morning of the school year — no exceptions. Same discipline as a hospital: the power has to be there when it's needed, or the whole thing stops. That's where this is heading. Electrification is putting critical-load demands on buildings and fleets that never had them before — and most of them are being wired by people learning it for the first time. We've been doing the hard version of this for 119 years. We're not new to power that can't fail. We're just getting started on where it's needed next.
NotePlants the forward flag on the category — critical power expanding through electrification — without naming a specific target. Deliberately does not say "data centers": that ambition is still unconfirmed with Mike. If he confirms it, this post gets a sharper, named edge. Until then it claims the ground, not the destination.
